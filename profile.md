# Learner Profile

## Known baseline

- CET4 written score: **568**
- CET6 written score: **389**
- Has substantial prior school-English exposure.
- Current system should optimize for practical use rather than test preparation by default.
- Do **not** infer speaking or listening proficiency directly from CET scores; calibrate from actual performance.

## Current learning decision — 2026-09-08

The learner decided that a 30-minute lesson is too short to split effectively across vocabulary, listening, speaking, reading, and writing while the lexical foundation is still limited.

From **2026-09-08 through 2026-10-07**:

- default lessons should be **text-only**,
- suspend planned **listening** practice,
- suspend planned **speaking** practice,
- focus primarily on **vocabulary, chunks/collocations, sentence patterns, domain expressions, contextual reading, and written reuse**,
- reassess the plan **on or after 2026-10-08**.

Listening and speaking are deferred, not permanently abandoned. Do not automatically restore them before the checkpoint unless the learner explicitly requests them.

## Current lexical-learning preference

Do not reduce vocabulary study to isolated `English = Chinese` pairs.

Prefer:
- reusable chunks and collocations,
- common sentence patterns,
- technical/domain expressions,
- individual words when independently high-value.

For new items, normally provide:
- relevant Chinese meaning,
- useful collocation/grammar note when needed,
- natural contextual example,
- later retrieval/reuse in a different context.

The learner wants more lesson time spent **actually building the lexical base**, rather than rushing through many modalities in one session.

## Calibration from 2026-09-07

### Listening

- An approximately 80–100 word natural technical explanation was too difficult.
- The learner reported that almost all words felt unfamiliar or could not be segmented reliably in speech.
- Repetition alone did not solve the problem.
- Chinese explanation and much shorter English restored comprehension.

This evidence contributed to the decision to pause listening and build vocabulary first.

### Speaking

- Short prompted phrases can be produced after modeling.
- Longer spontaneous output causes retrieval breakdown and hesitation.
- `prioritize` was difficult to retrieve/pronounce fluently in a sentence.

This evidence contributed to the decision to defer speaking until the lexical base is stronger.

### Writing / prompting

Writing is currently stronger than spontaneous speaking.

Successful guided outputs included:
- `I suggest fixing the stability issues first.`
- `Prioritize fixing the stability issues.`

### Reading

Reading remains useful mainly as a source of fresh context and vocabulary. Keep comprehension testing finite so it does not crowd out lexical learning.

## Voice preferences kept for later reassessment

These preferences remain relevant when Voice eventually returns:

### Chinese Voice
- Prefer clear Mainland Standard Mandarin / standard Putonghua.
- Strong regional accent can reduce comfort and intelligibility.
- Accent steering is best-effort; switch Voice if necessary rather than repeatedly prompting the same Voice.

### English Voice
- For beginner listening, prefer clear General American English, slower-than-casual delivery, clear articulation, minimal fillers, and short sentences.
- Do not use free-form Voice as the sole canonical pronunciation reference for individual words.

These Voice settings are **inactive by default during the vocabulary-foundation phase**.
